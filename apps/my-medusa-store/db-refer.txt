Failsafe Sequence: It unlinks downstream foreign keys (variants, price sets, channel links) before dropping parent option rows, completely eliminating PostgreSQL constraint errors.

Preserves Store Defaults: It completely leaves global catalogs (product_category, product_shipping_profile, sales_channel) and your migration tables untouched. Your foundational store setup stays completely intact!

Soft-Deleted Tables (Has deleted_at column): product, product_variant, product_variant_price_set, product_variant_inventory_item. When you delete a product via standard workflows, Medusa updates their timestamps to keep historical links alive.

Hard-Deleted Tables (No deleted_at column): product_sales_channel, price, price_set, inventory_item, product_option_value. These tables do not track history. If an active product uses them, deleted_at won't even exist as a column. If they are left behind with active records after a product is gone, they are pure, unlinked orphans.
exactly how Medusa was designed to behave. 

This layout cleanly handles the deep-cascading soft-delete strategy, ensuring every associated record—from pivot links to isolated module entries—receives a matching timestamp uniformly while avoiding loose database updates.

Soft Deletes vs. Hard Deletes: Medusa Core uses soft deletes (deleted_at timestamps) for complex entities like Products, Orders, and Links to avoid accidental orphan states. However, join tables or calculation adjustments (like line item discounts or tax lines) apply hard deletes (ON DELETE CASCADE) to instantly wipe the slate clean if a parent item leaves the cart.
